Reading the four cases. If r > g, any gap between the actual and the steady-debt deficit grows over time: debt runs away upwards, or falls away, unless the deficit adjusts. If r < g, growth pulls debt towards one level from either side. These are the four panels A to D of Figure 6 in the CORE Insight.
Real or nominal. The equation works with real r and real g, or with nominal i and nominal growth: inflation cancels out of the difference. In the nominal view the simulator uses r = i − π. Only unexpected inflation lowers the real burden of a debt already issued.
What this leaves out. d, r and g are held fixed for the whole projection. In reality a rising debt can push up r, a recession lowers g and raises d, and governments change policy. The equation is the approximation used in the CORE Insight; the exact form divides (r − g) by (1 + g).
Data. CORE Insights public debt simulator (Eichengreen and Panizza): debt-to-GDP in 2021; primary deficit and real growth averaged over 2000–2019; interest rates and inflation are the simulator's assumptions, not forecasts.
